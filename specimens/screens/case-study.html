Overview/Who uses it/Kastrup

How 340 editors stopped asking each other what an icon was called

A municipal administration with fourteen sites, one installation and no shared vocabulary. What changed, what it cost, and the two departments it did not work for.

Whether this is you

The shape of the deployment before any of the story, so a reader can stop here if it is nothing like theirs. Everything in the table is a fact somebody at Kastrup could dispute.

What This deployment
Sector Municipal administration
People affected 340 editors across 22 departments
Sites 1 installation, 14 sites, 3 languages
Release TYPO3 13.4, upgraded from 11.5
Agency Nordlys Digital, Aarhus
Ran from January to July 2026
What it replaced A wiki and two shared spreadsheets

What it was like before

Twenty-two departments had each grown their own names for the same things. A page-tree icon meant “unpublished” in one and “scheduled” in another, and both were right within their own department.

The cost was not the confusion — it was the asking. Every new editor spent their first month finding out who to ask, and every experienced one spent a slice of every week answering.

Two departments' folder structures side by side, with the same icons used for different states

The objection that nearly stopped it

The security office had one question, and it was the right one: a tool that reads the whole installation is a tool that can leak the whole installation.

What changed, and how it was measured

Four figures, each with what it was measured against and over how long. The fourth is the one that did not go our way, and it is here for the same reason the first three are.

to answer a naming question
Down from two days. Measured as the time from question asked in the team channel to a linked answer, over 61 questions between March and June.

The remaining 29% were about this installation’s own conventions, which the tool does not index.

It reads. The audit at the end of the pilot found no write path, which is what the security office needed before the rollout.
1 of 3
departments still on the old way
Stated because it is true: the two with their own conventions found the answers less useful and were not pushed.

What it took

Seven months, of which the first three were spent not on the tool at all but on agreeing which department’s names were going to win. That is the work this makes visible rather than the work it saves.

It does not index your own conventions
The two departments it did not help were the two with the most local vocabulary. Reading a site package for its own naming is on the roadmap — it was not available here, and this page is not going to pretend it was.

Other deployments

Two more, both smaller and neither in public administration. A single reference proves a single thing.
